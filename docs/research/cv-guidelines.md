# CV Guidelines (research draft)

**Purpose:** a numbered rule set (G01..G24) that the Review prompt cites in each Finding and the Rewrite prompt follows, for English-language CVs and résumés, region-neutral (v1).
**Retrieved:** 2026-10-08. Quotes in "double quotes" are verbatim from PDFs extracted locally with `pdftotext` (MIT Resume Guide, Europass instructions). Every other source line is a paraphrase of a page read through WebFetch, which summarises pages and may reword them. Treat those as paraphrases, not quotes.

**Invariant for every rule (product rule):** the Rewrite never adds a number, employer, title, tool, skill, date, outcome or contact detail that is not in the candidate's CV. Where a rule asks for missing information, the Rewrite inserts a bracketed placeholder (`[X%]`, `[result]`, `[team size]`, `[dates]`) and the Review states what the candidate must supply.

**Source key.** [2+] means at least two independent institutions support the rule. The MIT CAPD web pages and the MIT PDF count as one institution.
- HAR: Harvard FAS Mignone Center, https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/

- MIT-PDF: MIT CAPD Resume Guide, https://cdn.uconnectlabs.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2021/06/Resume_Guide.pdf
- MIT-ATS: MIT CAPD, https://capd.mit.edu/resources/make-your-resume-ats-friendly/
- NCS: UK National Careers Service, https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections

## Draft Guidelines

### Content & impact

**G01: Describe achievements and results, not duties.** [2+]
Rule: Each experience entry should say what the candidate accomplished or changed. A list of responsibilities is not enough. When a bullet has no outcome, the Rewrite adds a `[result]` placeholder and never writes an outcome itself.
Check: Bullets that only restate a job description ("Handled customer emails", "Maintained database") and give no outcome, contribution or effect.
Sources: MIT-W (accomplishments and contributions, not just responsibilities; descriptions should not read like job descriptions); MIT-PDF "Emphasize your accomplishments, contributions, and outcomes."; HAR (lists "Not demonstrating results" among the top five mistakes); USF (use action verbs instead of task descriptions; incorporate results); IMP (STAR, ending in Result).

**G02: Quantify only with numbers the candidate supplied.** [2+]
Rule: Where scale or impact can be measured (people, money, time, volume, %), express it as a number taken from the CV. If the CV has no number, the Rewrite inserts a placeholder such as `[X%]` or `[team size]` and never estimates one.
Check: Claims of size or impact with no figure ("large team", "significantly improved", "many clients", "budget").
Sources: MIT-PDF "Quantify bullet points to convey size and/or scale of project, budget, team, time or results."; MIT-W (quantify if you can; attendees, money raised or managed); HAR (fact-based: quantify and qualify); EUP "If necessary, quantify your responsibilities".

**G03: Be specific about what was done and how.** [2+]
Rule: Replace general statements with the concrete action, method or tool the CV already mentions. When the CV gives no specifics, flag the gap and do not supply any.
Check: Vague verbs or objects ("helped with projects", "worked on various tasks", "involved in"), or a bullet that names a task with no method.
Sources: MIT-W (include how you performed tasks, not just what you did); HAR (specific rather than general).

**G04: Evidence skills; don't just list them.** [2+]
Rule: Every skill the CV claims as a strength should be backed by at least one experience entry that shows it in use. The Rewrite may move or link existing evidence and must never add a skill.
Check: Skills in a skills list or profile (e.g. "leadership", "Python") that appear in no experience, project or education entry.
Sources: USF (showcase skills and competencies, not merely list them); MIT-PDF "Give evidence of your skills and personal impact"; IMP (make skills evident with a summary followed by STAR examples).

**G05: Tailor by selecting and ordering, never by adding.** [2+]
Rule: When a target role is given, put the entries most relevant to it first and give them more space, using only content already in the CV.
Check: With a target role supplied, the most relevant experience is buried below less relevant material or gets fewer details. Without a target role, this rule does not apply.
Sources: MIT-W (target a specific position; select experiences that show the required skills); NCS (match the CV to the job and company; review the advert); HAR (tailor to the type of position); EUP "highlight your advantages according to the specific requirements of the prospective employer."

**G06: Mirror job-posting terms only where the CV truthfully supports them.** [2+]
Rule: If the posting uses a term for something the CV already shows in other words, the Finding advises the candidate: "if accurate, consider the posting's term X". The Rewrite never applies a posting term itself; it keeps the CV's wording, and only the candidate decides whether the term is true. Never insert a keyword for a skill or tool the CV does not evidence, and never stuff keywords.
Check: A required term is missing even though the CV describes the same thing in other words (advise the candidate to consider the posting's term), or keyword lists appear that nothing else in the CV supports.
Sources: MIT-ATS (use posting keywords meaningfully; avoid spamming; you must be able to account for everything on the resume); MIT-W (employers may use keyword scanning); ILL (borrow wording from the job posting).

**G07: Cut irrelevant, repetitive or out-of-date content.** [2+]
Rule: Recommend removing material that adds nothing for the target reader. Rewrites may only delete or shorten here, never replace with new content.
Check: Superseded items (e.g. school-leaving qualifications next to a degree), duplicated bullets, or old, unrelated roles that take up as much space as relevant ones.
Sources: MIT-PDF "Omit unrelated memberships and information that is repetitive, implicit (e.g. high school graduation for a college graduate), or out-of-date."; EUP "work experience or training which is old or not relevant for the application can be omitted."; NCS (remove outdated information).

### Language & style

**G08: Start each bullet with a strong action verb.** [2+]
Rule: Experience bullets begin with a verb that names the candidate's contribution.
Check: Bullets that open with a noun, an article, "I", or a weak verb ("Worked", "Helped", "Did").
Sources: MIT-W (start with a strong action verb such as built, managed, developed); DUKE (begin with a strong, active verb); MIT-PDF "Use action verbs"; HAR (use action words, not passive language).

**G09: Remove duty phrases.** [2+]
Rule: Remove filler openers and start with the action instead.
Check: "Responsible for", "Duties included", "Hired to", "Project involved", "Tasked with".
Sources: MIT-PDF "Leave out unnecessary words, sentences, and phrases e.g. "Duties included / Hired to / Project involved / Responsible for..."; DUKE (avoid "responsible for" and "duties include").

**G10: Use no personal pronouns.** [2+]
Rule: Write in implied first person, with no "I", "me", "my", "we", "he" or "she".
Check: Any first- or third-person pronoun in a bullet or profile.
Sources: MIT-PDF "Don't use the first person, I, or any pronouns."; HAR (do not use personal pronouns such as I or We); DUKE; ILL.

**G11: Match verb tense to whether the role is current.** [2+]
Rule: Use present tense for roles and activities that are ongoing and past tense for those that have ended. Keep the tense consistent within each entry.
Check: Past-tense bullets under a role dated "to present", present-tense bullets under an ended role, or mixed tenses within one entry. If the CV does not say whether a role is current, flag it and do not guess.
Sources: DUKE (present tense for current activities, past tense for past ones); ILL (keep verb tenses consistent; present tense for current roles); USF (present tense for current positions, past tense for previous ones).

**G12: Prefer active voice.** [2+]
Rule: Rewrite passive constructions so the candidate is the implied subject.
Check: "was/were + past participle" constructions ("Reports were prepared", "was awarded by the team to…") where the candidate's role is unclear.
Sources: HAR (active rather than passive; lists passive language among the top five mistakes); MIT CAPD, https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-tips-avoid-the-passive-voice/ (eliminating passive voice helps; the full guide it links to is off-site and was not read).

**G13: Remove self-praise; let the facts show quality.** [2+]
Rule: Delete unsupported self-assessments rather than strengthening them. The Rewrite must not replace them with invented evidence.
Check: Self-descriptions such as "highly skilled", "outstanding", "excellent", "passionate", "world-class" with no evidence attached.
Sources: MIT-PDF "Avoid self-flattering terms such as "highly skilled, outstanding, or excellent." … let readers decide for themselves that you are well-qualified."; HAR (written to express, not impress; articulate rather than "flowery").

**G14: Write concise phrases, not narrative.** [2+]
Rule: Bullets are short phrases, and complete sentences or paragraphs are not required. Keep each role's description brief.
Check: Paragraph-length bullets, storytelling ("In this role I was able to…"), or multi-clause sentences that could be split or trimmed.
Sources: HAR (no narrative style; written for people who scan quickly); MIT-W (resumes don't require complete sentences); EUP "use short sentences"; NCS (usually 2 to 3 lines per role).

**G15: Avoid slang, jargon and repetition.** [2+]
Rule: Use plain professional wording, vary verbs, and do not repeat the same phrase across bullets.
Check: Colloquialisms, unexplained internal jargon, or the same verb or phrase opening several bullets in a row.
Sources: MIT-PDF "Avoid repeating words or phrases, as well as using jargon"; HAR (no slang or colloquialisms); ILL (use a variety of action verbs).

### Structure & sections

**G16: Include contact details at the top.** [2+]
Rule: The CV must give the candidate's name and a way to reach them (at least email and phone). Which other personal fields belong there is region-specific (see Deferred). The Rewrite never generates contact data and uses `[email]` or `[phone]` placeholders instead.
Check: No email or phone number found, or contact details appear somewhere other than the header.
Sources: HAR (lists missing email and phone among the top five mistakes); MIT-PDF "Your contact information is listed at the top of your resume"; NCS (include a contact details section); MIT-W.

**G17: Give every entry an organisation, a title and dates, newest first.** [2+]
Rule: Each experience and education entry names the organisation, the role or qualification, and its dates, and entries within a section run in reverse chronological order. The Rewrite flags missing fields with placeholders (`[employer]`, `[dates]`).
Check: Entries missing an employer, title or dates, or a section where an older entry comes before a newer one.
Sources: NCS (list the most recent experience first; include employer, job title, dates and what you did); HAR (reverse chronological within headings).

**G18: Order sections by relevance to the reader.** [2+]
Rule: Put the sections that best support the application first. For candidates with limited work experience, education may come before experience.
Check: A strong, relevant section (e.g. the degree for a new graduate, or relevant experience for an experienced hire) placed after weak or minor sections.
Sources: HAR (list headings in order of importance); EUP (describe education and training first when work experience is limited); MIT-ATS (position sections to reflect the posting's priorities).

**G19: Keep content in parseable text (limited check).** [2+]
Rule: Key content should not depend on tables, text boxes, columns, graphics or icons, because applicant tracking systems may drop or reorder it.
Check: Visible only as extraction artifacts in the CV text: interleaved columns, out-of-order fragments, orphaned labels, or empty sections. Flag the artifact; do not speculate about the original layout.
Sources: MIT-ATS (graphics, text boxes and tables may be distorted, ignored or erased; columns caused out-of-order text); ILL (special fonts, graphics or unusual layouts may be rejected by ATS scanners); DUN (complex designs and tables may not pass ATS); MIT-PDF "avoid using colors or graphics, which could affect how an applicant tracking system interprets your resume."

### Consistency & accuracy

**G20: Format dates, styles and grammar consistently.** [2+]
Rule: Use one date format, one bullet style, one way of emphasising text and one grammatical pattern throughout the CV.
Check: Mixed date formats ("Jan 2023", "01/2023", "2023-01"), inconsistent capitalisation of headings or titles, or bullets that switch between phrase styles.
Sources: HAR (be consistent in format and content); MIT-PDF "Be consistent and use the same grammatical style throughout."; DUKE (consistent styling and formatting across all sections); NCS (use the same style throughout).

**G21: Eliminate spelling, grammar and punctuation errors.** [2+]
Rule: Correct errors and keep to one English variant (US or UK) throughout. The Rewrite fixes spelling but never changes proper nouns, product names or job titles.
Check: Misspellings, typos, agreement errors, or mixed variants in one document ("organise" alongside "organize").
Sources: HAR (spelling and grammar errors are the first of the top five mistakes); MIT-PDF "Proofread one more time to ensure correct spelling, grammar and punctuation."; NCS (have someone check spelling and grammar); EUP "there is no excuse for mistakes in spelling and punctuation!"

**G22: Spell out acronyms on first use.** [2+]
Rule: Write the expanded form, then the acronym in parentheses, the first time an acronym appears, except for universally known ones. The Rewrite expands an acronym only when the expansion is unambiguous; otherwise it writes `[expand ACRONYM]`.
Check: Internal or domain acronyms (team names, tools, certifications) that are never expanded.
Sources: ILL (spell out acronyms the first time, followed by the acronym in parentheses); HAR (do not abbreviate). The two sources differ in strictness; see Rejected/contested.

**G23: Flag unexplained gaps; do not explain them.** [2+]
Rule: The Review points out unaccounted periods between dated entries. The Rewrite may add only a `[gap: reason]` placeholder and never invents a reason or an activity.
Check: Date ranges that leave several months unaccounted for between consecutive entries.
Sources: HAR (avoid information gaps such as a missing summer); EUP "explain any breaks in your studies or career"; NCS (says gaps are normal and links to advice on explaining them, so a gap finding is advisory, never a defect).

**G24: Keep every claim honest and verifiable.** [2+]
Rule: Never inflate titles, scope, skill levels or results. The Review flags claims that the CV's own content contradicts, and the Rewrite keeps the candidate's level of claim.
Check: Internal contradictions, such as "led" for a role titled intern with no team mentioned, "expert" in a skill used only in a course project, or a skill level that conflicts with a stated level elsewhere.
Sources: MIT-PDF "Be honest and accurate, but not overly modest."; EUP "do not artificially inflate your CV; if you do, you are likely to be found out at the interview."; MIT-ATS (be able to account for everything; falsification may lead to dismissal from the search); DUKE (evaluate language skills honestly).

## Region-specific rules deferred to v2

| Topic | US-leaning sources | UK/EU sources | Disagreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | MIT-PDF "Stick to 1-page in length" (2 pages for an advanced degree or 10+ years); DUKE (keep to one page); ILL (one page typically most appropriate) | EUP "in most cases, two pages are enough"; DUN (one or two full pages, avoid half pages) | 1 page vs up to 2 |

## Rejected or contested advice

- **"75% of résumés are auto-rejected by ATS."** Excluded. Only vendor and blog sources both make and dispute this claim (search results only; none fetched, and none count as reputable sources). MIT-ATS says some systems "rate or score" applicants and never says they reject automatically. G19 is therefore framed as a parsing risk, not as rejection.
- **Generic vs "standard" section headings.** MIT-PDF advises against generic headings ("Employment, or Other") in favour of descriptive ones ("Related Experience"). The common counter-advice to use standard headings for ATS appeared only in vendor content, which was not fetched. Not adopted in either direction.
- **Profile or summary statement.** IMP calls a profile statement optional and controversial. No rule requires or bans one.
- **Abbreviations.** HAR says not to abbreviate at all, while ILL says to spell out on first use. G22 follows ILL because a reviewer can check it in text.
- **Fonts, sizes, margins, white space, bold, colour, templates, file type** (HAR, MIT-W, MIT-PDF, NCS, MIT-ATS). Excluded because the Review sees extracted text and cannot verify these. Only the text-visible part survives, in G19.
- **Verb-strength rankings** (DUKE rates "Worked" good, "Managed" better, "Spearheaded" best) and **"use strong adjectives"** (MIT-PDF). Excluded. The rankings are stylistic, and "strong adjectives" conflicts with G13 when the adjectives are unsupported.
- **"Use numbers instead of writing them out"** (DUKE). Consistent with G20, but a single source and minor, so not a separate rule.
- **Tense statistics from Simplify** (vendor analysis showing many current-role bullets use past tense). Excluded as vendor data. G11 follows the university consensus.
